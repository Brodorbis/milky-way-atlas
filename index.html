Educational goal

The goal of this atlas is to make the Milky Way something you can explore, not just read about. Every one of its 281 entries is a real object placed from real measurements, from the black hole at the galaxy’s center to the planets next door, and every entry links to further reading. The notes on science fiction are a doorway: a story can spark curiosity about a star, and the science can keep that curiosity going. I hope it helps students, stargazers and anyone who has looked up and wondered what is out there.

About the creator

I’m Brody Ramirez, a student at The University of Texas at Austin majoring in Geosystems Engineering, and I aspire to become an astronaut. Geosystems engineering is about what lies beneath a planet’s surface: rock, water and energy, and how to find and use them. Those same questions will matter on the Moon and Mars, where future explorers will need to find water and build. This atlas is part of how I’m learning about the places I hope humanity will reach, and a way to share that excitement with anyone who wants to come along.

Research current as of 2026

The research, measurements and discoveries in this atlas reflect published science as of 2026. Astronomy moves quickly: new planets are confirmed, distances are refined, and some candidates turn out not to exist. Values marked “~” are estimates, and planets labeled candidate or disputed are not confirmed. For the newest findings, follow the Wikipedia links and the research they cite.
